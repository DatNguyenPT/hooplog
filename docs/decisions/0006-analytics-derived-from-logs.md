# 0006: Analytics are derived from the logs, and the charts are plain Views

Status: accepted (M1 dashboard and analytics slice)

**Context.** The player wants to see how they are trending (field goal %, consistency, volume) and their personal bests. Every number can already be worked out from the shots, shooting sessions, workout sessions and sets that are logged. Charts in React Native usually mean adding a chart package and an SVG renderer, which the project rules say must be justified.

**Decision.**
- No new table and no migration. A read-only `analyticsRepository` returns the logs the screen needs and the pure functions in `analytics/domain/progress.ts` turn them into figures. Soft-deleted rows and uncertain shots are left out, so editing or deleting a log corrects every chart with no bookkeeping.
- The screen shows one window at a time, which the player chooses: a day, a week, a month, or the last 8 weeks. Day, week and month can step to the previous or next one (forward stops at the window that holds today) and jump back to Today; switching between them keeps the date being looked at. The 8 weeks always look back from today. Weeks run Monday to Sunday and months follow the calendar, in the player's local time. Timestamps stay UTC ISO strings in the database; the repository returns the logs inside the window (from its start up to, not including, the start of the next one) and the day or week a log falls in is worked out in code, using the date helpers of training plans. The window rules are pure functions in `analytics/domain/period.ts`.
- Three measures: FG% (made out of resolved shots), sessions (shooting sessions that have a shot plus workouts that have a set, counted in the day or week they started) and weight lifted (reps × kg over sets that have both; bodyweight, timed and distance sets add nothing). A summary card totals them for the window. A week or month adds a chart per measure with a bar for each day; the 8 weeks add one with a bar for each week; a single day has just the summary.
- Personal bests: the best shooting session by FG% among sessions with at least 20 resolved shots (a tie goes to the session with more shots, then the earlier one), the longest run of makes within one session (uncertain shots neither extend nor break it), and per exercise the heaviest weight, or the most reps for an exercise done without added weight. A deleted custom exercise keeps its record because history keeps its name.
- The charts are drawn with plain `View`s: one series in the theme's primary colour, bars standing on a baseline, no chart or SVG dependency. Each bar is a button with a full-sentence accessibility label, and the selected day or week is shown by a highlight band, a bold axis label and a text readout, never by colour alone. The range picker is drawn the same way, as a row of radio buttons with previous, next and Today buttons. A native date picker or a month-grid calendar is not used: it would need a native module (a new phone build and its own ADR) for a screen that is mostly about "this week, last week, this month"; a grid for jumping to any date can be added later if players ask for it.
- Plan adherence for past weeks (did I do what the plan said) is not part of this slice.

**Trade-offs.** There is nothing new to maintain, license-check or upgrade, and the bars are as accessible and as easy to test as any other screen element. The cost is that line charts, zoom or animation would be extra work; if the product needs them later, a chart library gets its own ADR. Deriving from rows is always correct but repeats work each time the screen opens. The window's figures read only the logs inside it (never more than 8 weeks) and the lift bests are SQL aggregates, but the shooting bests read every shot ever logged, in order and grouped by session, because a streak depends on shot order. That is fine for thousands of shots; if it ever feels slow, a cached stats row per session can replace the scan without changing the screen. The 20-shot minimum is a product choice that stops "1 of 1" from being a record, and lives in one constant.

**Consequences.** `docs/TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE.md` section 4 points here. Analytics reads the week helpers of training plans and the exercise mapping and labels of workouts (reuse only: those features do not depend on analytics). Changing time zone moves which day or week a log falls in, as with "today" in ADR 0005; stored logs are not rewritten. All text on the screen goes through the `progress.*` translation keys.
